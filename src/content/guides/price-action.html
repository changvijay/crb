Open any trading app and you will see lines, bars, colours and sometimes ten indicators stacked on one chart. It looks complicated. Many traders solve this by removing almost everything and focusing on one thing: the price itself. That approach is called price action.

What you will learn

  • How to read candles and what they say about buyers and sellers
  • Where price tends to stop, bounce or break
  • How to tell a real move from a fake one
  • How to manage risk and control your emotions

1. What is price action?

Price action is the practice of reading raw price movement (candles, highs, lows and the levels where price reacts) to make trading decisions. You rely on this instead of leaning mainly on indicators like RSI or MACD.

Why not just use indicators? Indicators are calculated from old price, so they show up late. Price is the original source.

The core idea: Price already carries everything: news, demand, supply and the mood of the crowd. So if you read how price behaves, you can see what buyers and sellers are doing. This is a belief, not a law of nature. It is simply a way of thinking that many traders find useful.

What a price action trader looks at

  • Candle shapes (engulfing, doji, hammer, pin bar)
  • Support and resistance
  • Trend structure (higher highs and higher lows, or the opposite)
  • Breakouts and retests
  • Volume
  • Chart patterns (triangles, flags, double top, double bottom)
  • Market structure, gaps, Fibonacci and pivot levels

Simple example: Imagine Nifty keeps bouncing from around 24,000 (an example level only) and then forms a bullish engulfing candle there. A price action trader reads this as buyers defending that level. They may look for a buy trade with a stop-loss just below it.

Why people use it

  • The chart stays clean and easy to read
  • It works on NSE/BSE stocks, forex and crypto, and on any timeframe
  • It gives clear entry, stop-loss and target levels
The limits

Price action is subjective, so two traders can read the same chart differently. It needs practice and proper risk management. It also does not ban indicators: some traders add one or two (like a moving average) on top, and keep price as the main focus.

2. The basics

Price

Price is the last rate at which a buyer and a seller agreed to trade. Price rises when buyers are more aggressive and falls when sellers are.

Candles

Each candle summarises one time period using four values: Open, High, Low and Close (OHLC).

  • Bullish candle: the close is above the open. Buyers won that period.
  • Bearish candle: the close is below the open. Sellers won that period.
  • Body: the thick part, from open to close.
  • Wicks (shadows): the thin lines. They show prices that were reached but could not hold.

Timeframes

One candle can represent 1 minute, 15 minutes, 1 hour, 1 day and so on.

TypeTimeframesBest used for
Lower1m, 5m, 15mIntraday trading, with more noise
Medium1h, 4hSwing entries
HigherDaily, WeeklyTrend direction and stronger levels

As a rule, a signal on a higher timeframe is more reliable than the same signal on a lower one.

Market participants

Buyers (bulls) push price up by buying at market price or at higher prices. Sellers (bears) push price down by selling at market price or at lower prices. Price moves in the direction of whichever side is more aggressive.

Smart money (institutions)

This means banks, mutual funds, FIIs/DIIs, hedge funds and large operators.

  • They trade in huge quantities, so they cannot enter or exit at a single price.
  • They often build positions slowly near lows and sell slowly near highs.
  • Clues they may leave behind are sudden volume spikes and sharp reversals.

You cannot see their exact trades on a chart. Treat this as reading clues, not as proof.

Retail traders

These are individual traders with small capital.

  • They often buy breakouts late and panic-sell near bottoms.
  • They often place stop-losses at obvious spots.
  • Some traders believe large players push price to those spots to trigger the stops. This is a popular idea but hard to prove, so see it as a possibility.
Key takeaway

Candles show what happened. Timeframes show the bigger picture. Participants help explain why price moves.

3. Candlesticks

A candle shows Open, High, Low and Close for one period. Its shape tells you who controlled that period.

Anatomy

  • Body: a large body means a strong move. A small body means indecision.
  • Wicks: a long upper wick means sellers pushed price back down. A long lower wick means buyers pushed price back up.

The rule

A pattern matters only at a key level (support, resistance or a zone) and only with confirmation. A nice-looking candle in the middle of nowhere means very little.

4. Support and resistance

  • Support: a level where buying stops a fall. Think of it as a floor.
  • Resistance: a level where selling stops a rise. Think of it as a ceiling.
  • Treat them as zones, not exact lines. The more times price reacts at a level, the more important it is.

Types

  • Horizontal levels: repeated highs and lows.
  • Role reversal: a broken support becomes resistance, and a broken resistance becomes support.
  • Psychological levels: round numbers like Nifty 24,000 or a stock at ₹500.
  • Dynamic levels: moving levels like trendlines and moving averages (the 20, 50 and 200 are popular).

Supply and demand zones

  • Demand zone: an area where strong buying made price rise sharply.
  • Supply zone: an area where strong selling made price fall sharply.
  • These are judged by how fast price moved away from the area, not by how many times it touched.

How to draw levels

  1. Start from the higher timeframe.
  2. Mark clear reaction points, not every small wick.
  3. Draw zones using the wick-to-body range.
  4. Remove levels that price has ignored repeatedly.

5. Trend structure

A trend is read from the sequence of swing highs (peaks) and swing lows (dips).

TrendStructureUsual bias
UptrendHigher highs and higher lowsBuy pullbacks
DowntrendLower highs and lower lowsSell rallies
SidewaysRoughly equal highs and lowsTrade the range or wait
  • Trendline: join two or more swing lows (uptrend) or swing highs (downtrend). A third touch confirms it.
  • Channel: parallel lines around the trend. Buy near the lower line and sell near the upper line.
  • The trend is weakening when: an uptrend fails to make a new high, or breaks the last higher low.
  • Trade in the direction of the higher timeframe trend whenever you can.

6. Breakouts and retests

A breakout is price closing beyond a key level. A retest is price returning to that level to check whether it holds.

True breakout vs false breakout

SignTrue breakoutFalse breakout (fakeout)
CandleStrong body, closes beyond the levelLong wick, closes back inside
VolumeHighLow or fading
Follow-throughNext candles continueQuick return into the range

Retest entry

  1. Price breaks the level with a strong close.
  2. Price pulls back to the broken level.
  3. The level holds (you see a rejection candle) and you take the entry.
  4. Your stop-loss goes on the other side of the level.

Points to remember

  • Stop hunts: price sometimes spikes past an obvious level to trigger stops, then reverses.
  • Waiting for a candle close and a retest reduces fakeout losses.
  • A retest does not always happen. Sometimes price just runs. Missing a trade costs less than taking a bad one.

7. Volume

Volume is the number of shares or contracts traded. It shows the strength behind a price move.

  • Price up and volume up: a healthy move.
  • Price up and volume down: a weak move that may reverse.
  • Breakout with high volume: more reliable.
  • Climax volume: huge volume after a long trend. This often means the move is exhausted.
  • Volume dry-up: very low volume during a pullback or sideways phase. A breakout often follows.
  • Volume at key levels: high volume with rejection confirms the level.

Compare volume with its recent average (for example the last 20 candles) instead of judging it alone.

Good to know

Indices like Nifty and Bank Nifty do not have real volume, so traders check the futures chart. In forex, most apps show tick volume (the number of price changes), which is only a rough guide.

8. Chart patterns

Price often draws repeating shapes because crowds behave in similar ways again and again.

Targets

  • For most patterns, add the height of the pattern to the breakout point (subtract it for bearish patterns).
  • For flags and pennants, use the length of the sharp move before the pause (the flagpole).
  • Always wait for the breakout to be confirmed. A pattern that has not broken out is only a guess.

9. Market structure

Market structure is the map of swing highs and swing lows. It shows who is in control.

TermMeaning
Swing high / lowA peak or dip with lower or higher candles on both sides.
Break of structure (BOS)Price breaks a swing point in the trend direction. The trend continues.
Change of character (CHoCH)Price breaks the last swing against the trend. The first sign of a reversal.
Liquidity sweepPrice spikes past highs or lows to trigger stops, then reverses.
Order blockThe last opposite candle before a strong move. A zone where big players may have entered.
Fair value gap (FVG)A three-candle imbalance. Price often returns to fill it.
Premium / discountAbove or below the 50% mark of a swing range. Sell in premium, buy in discount.

How to use it: trade in the direction of the BOS, and look for entries at order blocks or fair value gaps after a pullback.

An honest note

Terms like order block, liquidity sweep and fair value gap come from newer "smart money" style teaching. They are popular but less tested than classic ideas like support, resistance and trend. Use them as tools, not as guarantees.

10. Gaps

A gap is empty space between one candle's close and the next candle's open. In Indian stocks it usually appears at the 9:15 AM open after news, results or global market moves.

GapWhere it appearsMeaning
CommonInside a rangeUsually fills quickly
BreakawayStart of a trendA strong move begins
RunawayMiddle of a trendThe trend continues
ExhaustionEnd of a trendA reversal is likely

Gap strategies

  • Gap and go: a high volume gap that holds. Trade in its direction.
  • Gap fill: a weak, low volume gap. Trade back toward the previous close.
  • Wait for the first 15 minutes before acting on an opening gap.

11. Fibonacci and pivot tools

Fibonacci retracement

Draw it from a swing low to a swing high in an uptrend (and from high to low in a downtrend) to find where a pullback may end.

  • Key levels: 38.2%, 50% and 61.8%. Some traders also use 78.6%. The 50% level is not a true Fibonacci ratio, but traders use it widely.
  • Golden zone: 50% to 61.8%, where trend pullbacks often end.
  • Extensions: 127.2% and 161.8% are used for targets.
  • It works best when a Fibonacci level lines up with support or resistance.

Pivot points

These are levels calculated from the previous day's high, low and close. They are widely used for intraday trading.

Pivot = (High + Low + Close) / 3
  • Floor pivots: Pivot, S1, S2, S3 (below) and R1, R2, R3 (above).
  • Camarilla: tighter levels. Reversals are watched near S3 and R3, and breakouts beyond S4 and R4.
  • Price above the pivot suggests a bullish bias. Price below it suggests a bearish bias.

12. Trading strategies

A strategy is simply your plan: when to enter, where to place the stop, and when to exit.

StrategyIdeaEntryStop-loss
Trend followingTrade with the trendBreak of a swing high or lowBelow the last swing
PullbackBuy dips in an uptrendRejection at support, Fibonacci or EMABelow the pullback low
Range tradingBuy support, sell resistanceReversal candle at the range edgeOutside the range
ReversalCatch a trend changeCHoCH plus a pattern at a key levelBeyond the extreme

Opening range breakout (ORB)Trade the first 15 to 30 minute rangeBreak of that high or lowOpposite side of the range
Supply / demandTrade fresh zonesReaction at an untested zoneBeyond the zone

Pick one or two strategies, test them on charts, and stick to them. Switching between many strategies is a common reason beginners stay confused.

Putting it together: the top-down method

  1. Higher timeframe (daily or weekly): find the trend and the big levels.
  2. Medium timeframe (1h or 4h): check if price is near one of those levels.
  3. Lower timeframe (15m or 5m): look for a candle signal to enter.
  4. Confluence: the more things agree (level, trend, candle and volume), the stronger the trade.

Trading styles

  • Scalping: trades that last seconds to minutes.
  • Intraday: buy and sell within the same day.
  • Swing: hold for days to weeks.
  • Positional: hold for weeks to months.

Choose the style that fits your time, capital and temperament.

Order types

  • Market order: buy or sell right now at the best available price.
  • Limit order: buy or sell only at your price or better.
  • Stop-loss order: exits your trade automatically if price moves against you.

13. Risk management

You will be wrong often. Risk management makes sure being wrong does not destroy your account.

  • Risk per trade: 1% to 2% of your capital.
  • Stop-loss: place it at a logical level (beyond the structure) and never move it further away.
  • Risk-reward: aim for at least 1:2. At 1:2, you can win only 1 trade in 3 and still break even.
  • Daily loss limit: stop trading once you hit a set loss for the day.
  • Trailing stop: when price moves in your favour, move your stop behind it to lock in profit.
  • Journal: record entry, exit, reason, result and mistakes for every trade.

Position sizing

Quantity = (Capital × Risk %) / (Entry − Stop-loss)

Example: Capital ₹1,00,000 and risk 1% means ₹1,000 at risk. Entry is ₹200 and stop-loss is ₹190, so risk per share is ₹10. Quantity = 1,000 / 10 = 100 shares.

In real trading, brokerage, taxes and slippage (getting a slightly worse price) reduce profits, so results will be a little lower than the maths.

Reality check

SEBI studies on individual F&O traders keep showing the same result: a large majority lose money. The latest study (FY26) found about 88% of individual traders had net losses, and FY25 was around 91%. Leverage makes small mistakes very expensive. Practise on charts or with very small quantity first.

14. Trading psychology

Strategy is only part of the game. Your mind decides whether you follow it.

ProblemFix
FOMO (fear of missing out)Wait for your setup. There is always another trade.
Revenge tradingStop after a loss and follow your daily limit.
OvertradingSet a maximum number of trades per day.
Fear of pulling the triggerReduce your position size and trust tested rules.
Moving the stop-lossDecide the stop before entry and never change it.
GreedBook profit at planned targets.
  • Follow the process, not the result of one trade.
  • Review your journal every week.
  • Accept that losses are part of trading.

15. Common beginner mistakes

  • Trading a candle pattern with no level and no confirmation
  • Ignoring the higher timeframe trend
  • Drawing too many lines until the chart is unreadable
  • Entering a trade without a stop-loss
  • Risking too much on a single trade
  • Chasing a breakout after it has already run
  • Skipping the trading journal
  • Trading big event days (results, RBI policy, Budget, expiry day) without a plan, since price can move wildly
  • Going live with real money before practising

16. Practice plan and checklist

  1. Learn one concept at a time: candles first, then levels, then trend.
  2. Go back on old charts and mark levels and patterns (this is called backtesting).
  3. Paper trade or use a demo account for a few weeks.
  4. Start live with a very small amount.
  5. Keep a journal and review it weekly.

Checklist before every trade

  • What is the higher timeframe trend?
  • Is price at a key level?
  • Is there a candle or structure signal with confirmation?
  • Does volume agree?
  • Where is my stop-loss?
  • Is my reward at least twice my risk?
  • Is my position size within 1% to 2% risk?

Final words

Price action is a skill, not a shortcut. Like learning to ride a bike, you will fall a few times. The goal is to keep the falls small, learn quickly and keep going. Start simple, be patient and protect your capital first.
